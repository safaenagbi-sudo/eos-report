\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter presents the results of applying the methodology described
in Chapter~\ref{ch:method}. The sections follow the same order as the
pipeline: data preparation, feature selection, model benchmarking,
imbalance-strategy selection, hyperparameter tuning, final model
evaluation, comparison with baselines and sequence models, spatial
robustness, and district-scale inference. Each result is reported
objectively; broader interpretation is reserved for
Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}.

\section{Preprocessing and Feature Engineering}
\label{sec:preprocessing_feature_results}
% ============================================================

\subsection*{Preprocessing attrition}

The preprocessing pipeline progressively filtered the raw Sentinel-2
and crop-label dataset down to a clean monocrop time series.
Table~\ref{tab:pipeline_funnel} summarises the row and QUADKEY counts
at each stage.

The largest single reduction came from full-row deduplication
(987,660 rows, down 34\% from the filtered dataset), caused by
overlapping Sentinel-2 tile exports. Monocrop label filtering removed
mixed-crop entries, and gap filtering with interpolation produced the
final dataset of \textbf{21,865~QUADKEYs}, each with exactly 30
acquisition dates spanning Season~B~2025.

\subsection*{Feature engineering}

Fifteen vegetation indices were computed from the scaled Sentinel-2
bands, and four statistical summaries (mean, std, min, max) plus six
phenological descriptors (start of season, end of season, peak value,
amplitude, rate of increase, integral) were extracted per index per
QUADKEY, yielding a \textbf{96-feature} tabular matrix of shape
$21{,}865 \times 96$.

The feature-correlation heatmap
(Figure~\ref{fig:engineered_feature_correlation_heatmap}) confirms
substantial redundancy between index pairs that share spectral bands:
NDMI and LSWI are perfectly correlated (r~=~1.000), and NDMI and NBR
share r~=~0.991. This redundancy motivates the three-stage feature
selection in Section~\ref{sec:feature_selection_results}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figures/engineered_feature_correlation_heatmap.png}
    \caption{Pearson correlation matrix of the 96 engineered features.
    Several index pairs are near-perfectly correlated because they share
    spectral bands, confirming the need for a feature-selection step
    before model training.}
    \label{fig:engineered_feature_correlation_heatmap}
    \label{fig:corr_heatmap}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Stage 1 --- ANOVA $F$-test with Bonferroni correction.}
All 96 features passed the Bonferroni-corrected threshold
($\alpha / 96$), so no predictors were removed at this stage. Every
engineered variable carried at least some class-discriminative signal,
though the ANOVA filter does not address correlation.

\paragraph{Stage 2 --- Random Forest importance ranking.}
Feature importances placed moisture and dryness indices at the top:
NBR\_min, NBR2\_min, LSWI\_mean, NBR\_mean, and NBR\_eos were the five
highest-ranked features. Figure~\ref{fig:rf_feature_importance} shows
the top 15.

\begin{figure}[H]

    phenological timing features follow.}
    \label{fig:rf_feature_importance}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Stage 3 --- Recursive feature elimination (RFECV).}
RFECV with 5-fold cross-validation selected \textbf{37 features} as the
optimal subset, the point at which cross-validated macro F1 peaked
(Figure~\ref{fig:rfe_feature_selection_curve}). This 37-feature subset
was used for all subsequent modelling, imbalance-strategy testing, tuning,
and district-scale inference.

\section{Class-Imbalance Strategy Selection}
\label{sec:imbalance_results}
% ============================================================

Seven imbalance strategies were compared using 5-fold CV on the
training set, evaluated independently for each of the three top
models. The winning strategy was chosen by \textbf{average rank across
all three models}, so that the selected method would be consistently
strong rather than optimal for only one model family.

The strategies tested were: class weighting only, full SMOTE (auto),
per-class SMOTE light (40\% of RICE count), per-class SMOTE medium
(60\%), per-class SMOTE strong (80\%), per-class SMOTE at RICE level
(100\%), and ADASYN.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering

three top-benchmark models. Lower average rank is better.
Per-class SMOTE medium was selected.}
\label{tab:imbalance_strategy_summary}
\reporttablestyle
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{chaptercolor}
\reportth{Imbalance strategy} & \reportth{Average rank} &
\reportth{Mean CV macro F1} \\
\midrule
\rowcolor{accentgold!12}
Per-class SMOTE medium     & \textbf{1.67} & \textbf{0.9209} \\
Per-class SMOTE strong     & 2.67 & 0.9194 \\

Per-class SMOTE medium achieved the lowest average rank (1.67) and the

F1. The test set was not used at this stage.

The macro average precision (PR-AUC) was \textbf{0.9785}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{figures/best_model_precision_recall.png}

The gap between BEAN's ROC-AUC (0.9958) and PR-AUC (0.9447) is the

an imbalanced dataset: ROC-AUC is inflated by the large number of true
negatives, whereas PR-AUC focuses only on the minority class predictions.
PR-AUC is the more honest indicator of BEAN classification quality.

probability values, and the threshold $\tau_c$ that maximised the 
per-class F1 score was selected using \texttt{np.argmax}. Table~\ref{tab:per_class_thresholds} reports the resulting
thresholds used at inference time.

\section{Nyagatare District Inference}
\label{sec:nyagatare_inference_results}
% ============================================================

The final tuned Extra Trees model was applied to the complete Nyagatare
district using the Polars-based inference pipeline.

\subsection*{Data volume processed}

The raw Nyagatare Sentinel-2 dataset comprised 1,573,039,818~rows and
19,864,787~unique QUADKEYs. After the February--August~2025 filter,
749,535,814~rows remained. Quality control nullified 65.0\% of those
observations. After gap filtering, interpolation, vegetation-index
computation, feature extraction, and application of the Dynamic World
cropland mask, the inference matrix contained
\textbf{4,088,243~cropland QUADKEYs} and 37~selected features.

\subsection*{Prediction summary}

Each QUADKEY was assigned a crop label only if its predicted class
probability met the class-specific threshold from
Table~\ref{tab:per_class_thresholds}. QUADKEYs falling below the
threshold were labelled \texttt{uncertain}.
